# Selfward for Linux

A private reflection companion, ported from the iPhone app ([therAIpist](https://github.com/AnubisRooster/therAIpist), called Selfward on the phone). Everything runs on this laptop:
the model (Ollama), the database (SQLite) and the window. Built with the same shell as SmartWard: a Python API, a React interface and a small Tauri window with a tray icon.

> Selfward is **not** a licensed therapist, psychologist or medical provider. It is a journaling and self-reflection tool and cannot diagnose or treat anything.
> **Get help now** (always in the sidebar) lists the crisis lines for the country chosen in Settings.

## Help guide

Install steps, setup, voice, free models, phone access, updating and troubleshooting are in [HELP.md](HELP.md), also shown in the app: **Settings → Help guide**.

## Run

- Open **Selfward** from the applications menu (or `bin/start_selfward.sh`). It starts and watches the API (`http://localhost:8100`; SmartWard uses 8000, so both can run).
  Closing the window keeps it in the tray; tray → Quit stops it.
- Browser version: `SELFWARD_BROWSER=1 bin/start_selfward.sh`. `bin/start_selfward.sh stop` stops the API.
- Data lives in `~/.local/share/selfward/` (SQLite, owner-only permissions). Logs: `~/.local/state/selfward/api.log`.
- First time on a machine: `scripts/build_ui.sh`, `scripts/build_native.sh`, then `scripts/install_desktop.sh` (menu entry and icons).

## What carried over from the iPhone app (phase 1)

- **Three guides** sharing one memory: the AI Reflection Guide (unnamed by default; 15 approaches: integrated, free form, CBT, DBT, ACT, psychodynamic, humanistic, existential, Gestalt, somatic, narrative, IFS, Adlerian, Jungian, active imagination), the Companion (7 personalities, 4 presences, a name) and the Spiritual Advisor (9 traditions, a name). The prompts are copied from the Swift source by `scripts/port_prompts.py`.
- **Safety, in code, not in a prompt:** crisis phrases and requests for self-harm methods are answered locally with the crisis lines for your country and never reach the model; replies that diagnose, prescribe or push a faith are replaced and logged. Settings → Safety log shows what was caught.
- **Memory:** each exchange is embedded (Ollama, `nomic-embed-text`) and recalled in later sessions, across guides. Without the embedding model it falls back to matching words.
- **Inner Map** (sidebar): the feelings, people, beliefs and events you write about, and how they connect, as a map or a list; filter by kind or session, click a thing to see its connections and the sessions it came up in; export GraphML (Gephi) or Cytoscape JSON. It is built from your own words with plain keyword rules, no model, and crisis messages are kept out. Differences from the phone: variants of one feeling are one node (Anger, not Angry and Anger), words inside other words don't match, a belief is one clause, and the phone's "cycles" are left out because the rules link every feeling and belief in a message both ways, so they were not real patterns. "Rebuild from my sessions" builds the map from sessions written before it existed.
- **Insights** (sidebar): *Highlights* read the map aloud ("You often feel anxiety when Mother comes up"); *Dreams* are saved when you tell one in a session ("I had a dream…"), with the feelings and symbols picked out, or added by hand, and "Offer a reading" asks your model for a tentative Jungian reading (crisis wording is never sent; the reply gets the same boundary check as chat); *Notes* include one summary per session that starts after your second message and updates until you edit it, plus your own. Chat messages show a small "Dream saved" / "Note started" badge. All of it is found in your own words with plain rules, so it also works when the model is down, and "Look in older sessions" fills in sessions written before it existed.
- **Memory you can see** (Settings → What Selfward remembers): besides every exchange, moments that carry weight (a parent, a loss, shame, a realisation, "growing up…") are kept for good and brought up in later sessions when they are relevant, under their own heading. Only your own words decide what is kept (not the guide's reply), words are matched whole, and you can add your own, forget any, or switch it off. Deleting a session deletes the memories it created.
- **Long chats:** the prompt is sized to the model's window (Settings → Model → Conversation window, 8,192 tokens by default, also sent to Ollama as `num_ctx`, because Ollama's own default is small and silently cuts the start of a long prompt, where the guide's instructions are). When a chat outgrows it, the newest turns stay word for word and older exchanges that bear on what you just wrote are brought back from this chat's own memory. After each answer, older turns are folded into a short recap by the model (never before it, so you don't wait; on a CPU-only laptop a recap can take minutes), and the next long turn uses it. A recap that is not about the conversation (a complaint about the input, a refusal) is thrown away; if the model is down the chat just trims. Measured with llama3.2 3B on this laptop: it recalled a dog's vet advice and a class from 20 turns back through the in-chat memory, while the recap alone kept themes but dropped specifics, which is why both exist.
- **Narrative** (sidebar): a life story your model writes from your notes, dreams (as you told them) and the memories Selfward kept, then revises with only the new material each time you press Update (it can take minutes on a laptop; it runs in the background). Falls back to your own messages when there are no notes or dreams. It describes what you wrote, not who you are; small models embellish, so you can edit the text, go back one version, export it as Markdown, or delete it. A rewrite that sounds like a diagnosis, is a refusal, doesn't carry over what the sources say, or drops most of the existing story is held back and the story stays as it was (the phone replaced the whole story with a refusal sentence). With a cloud chat model, anything with crisis wording is never sent, and nothing after it either, until you tick "Write it on this computer"; sending anything to a cloud model asks first. Deleting a session does not edit a story already written from it; the delete dialog says so.
- **OpenRouter free models** (Settings → Model → OpenRouter free models): lists the models OpenRouter offers at no cost (a public list, fetched only when you open it, no key sent), with search, context size, a Test button and a Use button. The top entry, **Automatic**, is OpenRouter's own free router, which picks a free model that is answering; individual free models are often rate-limited, gated to your account, or only work inside coding agents, and the errors say so in plain words. Choosing one asks first, because what you write in sessions then leaves this computer and free providers may keep it. Memory, the Inner Map, Insights, the crisis check and your journal stay local; the Narrative and dream readings ask before sending anything. Your key is stored only on this computer, is never shown back, and is left out of backups and errors.
- **Voice** (Settings → Voice): every reply has *Read aloud*; the microphone button in a session (or **Talk** on the Sessions list) turns on hands-free: it listens, sends each sentence when you pause, reads the answer, and listens again. It never listens while it speaks, switches itself off when the window is hidden, the app locks, or you leave the session, and the microphone is open only while it is on. Say "stop" to interrupt or "stop listening" to finish. Each guide can have its own voice. Spoken words go through the same crisis check as typed ones (checked: a spoken crisis sentence is transcribed, answered with the crisis lines at once, and never reaches the model). The voice (Kokoro) and the listening (Whisper) run on this computer and are SmartWard's, found in its folder (about 1 GB, not copied; set `SELFWARD_VOICE_HOME` to use another folder with `tts/` and `stt/`); the speech server starts by itself when needed. The folder is deliberately not a setting. Without them the browser's own voice reads aloud and hands-free is unavailable.
- **A model per session:** the small chip under a session's title (a chip with a chip icon is on this computer, a cloud icon is not) opens a picker: follow the default, any model installed in Ollama, the free OpenRouter models, or any other model id. It takes effect from your next message and the context comes along, so you can start on the model on this computer and move to a bigger one (or back). Moving to a cloud model asks first, because the earlier messages, the recap and recalled memories go with it, and a cloud model is tested before the switch so a wrong key or a busy free model can't leave the session broken. Each reply says which model wrote it.
- **Mood** (sidebar): tap how you feel right now (1 very low … 5 great), optionally with a word or two, as often as you like; a line of your days (daily averages, 2 weeks / month / 3 months) builds up, with the single check-ins as faint dots and a list you can delete from. Days are your computer's local days. Check-ins and notes stay in your journal and are never given to a model. If a note has wording that calls for support, the check-in is still saved and the crisis lines for your country appear at once. It is in backups, exports and Erase.
- **Like the phone app's sessions:** the guides' own portraits, an icon for each of the 15 approaches, a cpu or cloud mark for the model, and small pills under a reply for what it added (memories, nodes, edges, dream logged, note, insight saved) that open the page they belong to. The guide, the approach and the model can each be changed mid-session; every reply keeps the name of whoever wrote it. A session can be titled when it is started and renamed any time (the pencil by its title).
- Onboarding (notice you must accept, profile and intake that shape every prompt, country, default guide), PIN lock with a pause after repeated wrong PINs, light/dark/system theme,
  Markdown and JSON export, passphrase-encrypted backup and restore, erase everything, optional cloud model with an API key (clearly marked: then your words leave this computer).

## From your phone (Tailscale, no app store)

Settings → **Remote access** walks through it; it is off by default. Ported from SmartWard (same front door, same tests), with these differences. The API still listens only on `127.0.0.1`; [Tailscale Serve](https://tailscale.com/docs/features/tailscale-serve) publishes it to *your own devices only* (never Funnel, which is public).
Selfward uses its own https port so it can sit beside SmartWard: `tailscale serve --bg --https=8443 127.0.0.1:8100`, then `https://<laptop>.<tailnet>.ts.net:8443`. On the phone you sign in once with a remote passphrase, then Add to Home Screen.

- **The front door** (`sf/remote.py`): a request that came through the proxy must be from the allowed Tailscale account and Tailscale host name, carry a sign-in session (cookie `HttpOnly; Secure; SameSite=Strict`, stored hashed) and, for writes, come from the same site. Five wrong passphrases lock sign-in for five minutes. Requests whose `Host` isn't a loopback name are refused even locally (DNS rebinding). Replies to a phone are never cached.
- **Seven days**, not SmartWard's 30: a phone stays signed in for 7 days after you last use it. Changing the passphrase, or turning remote access off, signs every phone out.
- **What a phone can do:** everything in the app (you chose that), including settings, backup, export and erase, so keep the passphrase to yourself and sign out of a phone you lose. Two things stay on the laptop because a stolen phone could abuse them: managing remote access itself (so it can't keep itself signed in or lock you out) and the laptop's own microphone (so nobody can listen to the room). The laptop's PIN screen does not appear on a phone; the remote passphrase is its lock. Remote settings are never written through Settings and never carried in a backup or restore.
- **Talking from the phone:** the phone cuts what you say into sentences itself (`frontend/src/vad.ts`), sends each as a small WAV to `POST /api/voice/transcribe`, and the laptop's Whisper (SmartWard's `stt/worker.py`) turns it into text. Nothing goes to Apple or Google. Spoken words get the same crisis check as typed ones. Answers are spoken by the natural voice through `/api/tts`.
- **Availability:** works only while the laptop is awake and Selfward is running. "Keep this laptop awake" holds a `systemd-inhibit` lock while remote access is on; closing the lid still suspends it.
- **Tests:** `tests/test_remote.py` (each check in the front door was switched off in turn and a test failed every time), `tests/test_remote_panel.py`, `tests/test_voice.py`, `frontend/src/__tests__/phone.test.ts`. Not tested against real Tailscale (it is not installed on this laptop): the tests send the headers Tailscale Serve adds.

## Development

`scripts/check.sh` runs the Python tests (temporary data folder, a stubbed model: nothing touches your real journal), shell syntax, the TypeScript check and the front-end tests.
`scripts/check.sh --build` also rebuilds `ui/`; `--native` also runs `cargo check`. `scripts/smoke.sh` checks the running app.
